\noindent\textbf{Implementation Details.}
We use LLaVA-Med-1.5-mistral-7B \cite{Li2023LLaVAMed} as the base model. Med-SAM \cite{ma2024segment} with clinical query guidance is used to localize the lesion area. The visual encoder is CLIP ViT-L14 at input resolution 336. Optimization employs LoRA with rank 128 and scaling 256. Dropout is set to 0.05 and no LoRA bias is used. Training runs for three epochs with a per-device batch size of two and gradient accumulation of one. We set the learning rate to 1e-6 with cosine scheduling and a warmup ratio of 0.03. Weight decay is zero. We save at the end of each epoch, keeping a single retained checkpoint. Prompts and context formatting are kept consistent across methods. All experiments are conducted on 8 NVIDIA A100 GPUs.

\noindent\textbf{Baselines.}
We compare CaMedPO with DPO \cite{rafailov2023direct} and its variants on medical tasks, including STLLaVA-Med \cite{sun2024stllava} and MMedPO \cite{Zhu2025MMedPO}. Note that CaMedPO can be integrated with other DPO methods for improvement by modifying $\mathcal{L}_\text{DPO}$ in Eq. \ref{eq:final_loss}. We further compare our method against two preference optimization methods for natural image. Specifically, mDPO \cite{wang2024mdpo} serves as a direct preference optimization method, while SPPO \cite{wu2025selfplay} is a self-rewarding model. We integrate our causal preference optimization framework with both methods and use them as baselines to assess comparative performance. In addition, we also evaluate MMedPO and all baseline models after SFT. Further implementation details are provided in Appendix \ref{supp:baselines}.

\noindent\textbf{Evaluation Metrics.}
For VQA, we report normalized exact-match accuracy, where clinical correctness and hallucination are assessed with the Lingshu \cite{xu2025lingshu} evaluation framework MedEvalKit. For report generation, we report BLEU Score \cite{papineni2002bleu}, ROUGE-L \cite{papineni2002bleu}, and METEOR \cite{banerjee2005meteor}. Furthermore, to evaluate the clinical factual correctness of generated reports beyond textual overlap, we employ RadGraph \cite{jain2021radgraph} metrics to extract and match clinical entities and relations.

\subsection{Main Results}
We note that the SLAKE and VQA-RAD test sets used in MMedPO differ from the official splits. For fair comparison, we therefore re-evaluate LLaVA-Med \cite{Li2023LLaVAMed}, STLLaVA-Med \cite{sun2024stllava}, DPO \cite{rafailov2023direct}, and MMedPO \cite{Zhu2025MMedPO} using the Lingshu toolkit, while official results are retained for IU-XRay and MIMIC-CXR. CaMedPO is introduced as a causal extension of the unweighted MMedPO and is further applied to mDPO \cite{wang2024mdpo} and SPPO \cite{wu2025selfplay} to assess the generality of the proposed causal preference optimization framework.

\noindent\textbf{Comparison with Baseline Methods.}
As reported in Table \ref{tab:main_results}, CaMedPO achieves consistent improvements over all baseline preference optimization methods across both medical VQA and report-generation tasks. When compared with MMedPO \cite{Zhu2025MMedPO}, CaMedPO exhibits clear gains on multiple benchmarks. For example, on SLAKE, CaMedPO improves open-ended recall from 51.67\% to 53.11\% and closed-ended accuracy from 58.31\% to 71.16\%. On IU-XRay, CaMedPO also yields consistent gains, improving ROUGE-L from 29.52\% to 32.68\%. These representative improvements indicate that CaMedPO provides a more reliable preference signal, yielding better alignment of medical reasoning across diverse datasets.

\noindent\textbf{Comparison with Baseline Methods Enhanced by SFT.}
As shown in Table \ref{tab:main_results}, CaMedPO continues to outperform all SFT-enhanced baselines, with more improvements than in the non-SFT setting. Relative to MMedPO \cite{Zhu2025MMedPO} after supervised fine-tuning, CaMedPO achieves larger gains across both VQA and report-generation benchmarks. For example, on SLAKE-Closed, performance increases from 67.82\% to 74.40\%, and on VQA-RAD closed-ended accuracy from 67.73\% to 74.90\%. On IU-XRay, CaMedPO further improves ROUGE-L from 30.13\% to 34.13\%. These results suggest that SFT provides a stronger reference policy that amplifies the benefits of causal preference optimization. By decoupling background-related bias from the preference signal, CaMedPO is able to extract more reliable supervision from the improved SFT initialization.

\noindent\textbf{Applicability to Different Preference Optimization Algorithms.} Beyond enhancing MMedPO, the proposed causal adjustment is also applied to mDPO and SPPO to assess its generality. In both cases, the causal variants exhibit consistent improvements over their baselines. For mDPO \cite{wang2024mdpo}, CaMedPO increases SLAKE closed-ended accuracy from 60.29\% to 65.07\% and improves IU-XRay ROUGE-L from 22.38\% to 30.65\%. For SPPO, CaMedPO further raises VQA-RAD open-ended recall from 38.50\% to 41.00\% and achieves stronger report-generation results, using the second-round SPPO outputs for evaluation. These observations indicate that the proposed causal correction provides a transferable enhancement that strengthens preference optimization algorithms.

\noindent\textbf{Superiority in Clinical Grounding.} As shown in Table \ref{tab:radgraph}, CaMedPO achieves state-of-the-art performance across all RadGraph metrics and significantly outperforms baseline methods. Specifically, CaMedPO attains a Simple-F1 score of 35.23, surpassing the SFT baseline of 30.32 and the standard DPO score of 31.11. This improvement indicates that our causal preference optimization successfully encourages the model to generate factually accurate medical findings rather than hallucinated text.

\noindent\textbf{Comparison with MMedPO.} Notably, MMedPO yields a lower Simple-F1 score of 26.33 compared to standard DPO, despite its comparable performance in BLEU and ROUGE metrics shown in Table \ref{tab:main_results}. This suggests that while MMedPO optimizes for general medical language style through clinical relevance weighting, it may inadvertently sacrifice fine-grained factual precision. In contrast, CaMedPO explicitly constructs preference pairs based on lesion-masked counterfactuals. This forces the model to distinguish between visual evidence and background priors, ensuring that the generated entities are grounded in actual image content rather than spurious correlations.

\noindent\textbf{Analysis of Complex Relations.} The advantage of CaMedPO is consistent in the stricter Complete-F1 metric of 21.42 compared to 15.68 for MMedPO, which requires correct identification of both entities and their dependency relations. This confirms that our causal framework not only detects abnormalities but also accurately describes their attributes and locations, a capability that is crucial for trustworthy medical diagnosis.

\noindent\textbf{Validation of Causal Mechanism.} To explicitly verify that CaMedPO mitigates background shortcuts, we conducted controlled interventions at test time as shown in Table \ref{tab:causal_validation}. Regarding robustness to background shifts, we randomly perturbed non-lesion regions during inference. MMedPO suffers a significant performance drop where ROUGE-L falls from 30.13 to 25.21, indicating it relies heavily on spurious background cues. In contrast, CaMedPO maintains high performance with a score of 33.65, confirming that it has successfully learned to focus on lesion evidence rather than context. We further analyzed lesion-dependency by removing lesions from the input. CaMedPO performance drops more sharply than baselines, proving it relies on lesion features rather than guessing. Finally, better segmentation using MedSAM further enhances the causal contrast compared to MedKLIP, validating our pipeline design.

\subsection{Ablation Study}
In Table \ref{tab:ablation}, we select SFT-enhanced MMedPO as the CaMedPO baselines to conduct ablation studies on VQA-RAD and IU-XRay datasets. Multi-rounds training, sample ratio of causal pairs, and compatibility analysis on other LVLMs are in Appendix \ref{supp:cases}.

\noindent\textbf{Loss Function Variants.}
We evaluate several loss function variants to understand their contribution. Removing the standard DPO term leads to a clear performance drop (for example, VQA-RAD open-ended recall decreases from 42.00\% to 29.50\%), indicating that learning from factual preference pairs remains essential. Applying causal weights to the original DPO loss, where the details is in Appendix \ref{supp:cases} improves over MMedPO but remains weaker than the full model, as reflected by the IU-XRay ROUGE-L score of 28.40\% compared 
with 34.13\%. Removing the counterfactual clinical query provides only marginal improvements, thus it is not adopted in our causal preference optimization.

\noindent\textbf{Medical Visual Localization Tool.}
We further examine the impact of the lesion–background decomposition module by replacing the Med-SAM \cite{ma2024segment} localization with MedKLIP \cite{medklip}. CaMedPO$^{\text{s}}$ w/ MedKLIP leads to a moderate performance drop, where VQA-RAD open-ended recall decreases by 4\%, indicating that accurate lesion localization contributes to stronger causal contrast. Nevertheless, the model still outperforms the original MMedPO$^{\text{s}}$ baseline in all metrics, showing that even imperfect localization retains the advantage of causal preference construction.

As illustrated in Fig. \ref{fig:segmentation}, we analyze the impact of segmentation quality on test-time performance via IoU evaluation against annotated lesions on SLAKE, with accuracy computed over both open- and closed-ended questions. We find that inaccurate lesion segmentation interferes with preference signals and degrades performance, especially for stronger models. Meanwhile, better segmentation tools consistently improve overall performance, as higher IoU implies lower uncertainty. Critically, even when Med-SAM produces a lower IoU (indicating lower confidence), it still outperforms the MMedPO baseline, demonstrating the robustness of our method.
