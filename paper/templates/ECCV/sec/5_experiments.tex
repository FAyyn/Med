\subsection{Main Results}






We note that the SLAKE and VQA-RAD test sets used in MMedPO differ from the official splits. For fair comparison, we therefore re-evaluate LLaVA-Med \cite{Li2023LLaVAMed}, STLLaVA-Med \cite{sun2024stllava}, DPO \cite{rafailov2023direct}, and MMedPO \cite{Zhu2025MMedPO} using the Lingshu toolkit, while official results are retained for IU-XRay and MIMIC-CXR.
CaMedPO is introduced as a causal extension of the unweighted MMedPO and is further applied to mDPO \cite{wang2024mdpo} and SPPO \cite{wu2025selfplay} to assess the generality of the proposed causal preference optimization framework.




\noindent\textbf{Comparison with Baseline Methods.}
As reported in Table \ref{tab:main_results}, CaMedPO achieves consistent improvements over all baseline preference optimization methods across both medical VQA and report-generation tasks. When compared with MMedPO \cite{Zhu2025MMedPO}, CaMedPO exhibits clear gains on multiple benchmarks. For example, on SLAKE, CaMedPO improves open-ended recall from 51.67\% to 53.11\% and closed-ended accuracy from 58.31\% to 71.16\%. On IU-XRay, CaMedPO also yields consistent gains, improving ROUGE-L from 29.52\% to 32.68\%. These representative improvements indicate that CaMedPO provides a more reliable preference signal, yielding better alignment of medical reasoning across diverse datasets.

\noindent\textbf{Comparison with Baseline Methods Enhanced by SFT.}
As shown in Table \ref{tab:main_results}, CaMedPO continues to outperform all SFT-enhanced baselines, with more improvements than in the non-SFT setting. Relative to MMedPO \cite{Zhu2025MMedPO} after supervised fine-tuning, CaMedPO achieves larger gains across both VQA and report-generation benchmarks. For example, on SLAKE-Closed, performance increases from 67.82\% to 74.40\%, and on VQA-RAD closed-ended accuracy from 67.73\% to 74.90\%. On IU-XRay, CaMedPO further improves ROUGE-L from 30.13\% to 34.13\%. These results suggest that SFT provides a stronger reference policy that amplifies the benefits of causal preference optimization. By decoupling background-related bias from the preference signal, CaMedPO is able to extract more reliable supervision from the improved SFT initialization.

\noindent\textbf{Applicability to Different Preference Optimization Algorithms.} Beyond enhancing MMedPO, the proposed causal adjustment is also applied to mDPO and SPPO to assess its generality. In both cases, the causal variants exhibit consistent improvements over their baselines. For mDPO \cite{wang2024mdpo}, CaMedPO increases SLAKE closed-ended accuracy from 60.29\% to 65.07\% and improves IU-XRay ROUGE-L from 22.38\% to 30.65\%. For SPPO, CaMedPO further raises VQA-RAD open-ended recall from 38.50\% to 41.00\% and achieves stronger report-generation results, using the second-round SPPO outputs for evaluation. These observations indicate that the proposed causal correction provides a transferable enhancement that strengthens preference optimization algorithms.

\subsection{Clinical Factuality}
While standard N-gram metrics (e.g., BLEU, ROUGE) measure textual similarity, they fail to explicitly quantify the clinical correctness of generated reports. To address this, we employ RadGraph \cite{jord2021radgraph}, a specialized metric that evaluates the model's ability to extract and generate clinically meaningful entities (e.g., "pleural effusion", "upper lobe") and their relations (e.g., "located at").
Superiority in Clinical Grounding. As shown in Table \ref{tab:radgraph}, CaMedPO achieves state-of-the-art performance across all RadGraph metrics, significantly outperforming baseline methods. Specifically, CaMedPO attains a Simple-F1 score of 35.23, surpassing the SFT baseline (30.32) and the standard DPO (31.11). This improvement indicates that our causal preference optimization successfully encourages the model to generate factually accurate medical findings rather than hallucinated text.

Comparison with MMedPO. An interesting observation is that MMedPO$^{\mathrm{s}}$ yields a lower Simple-F1 score (26.33) compared to standard DPO (31.11), despite its comparable performance in BLEU/ROUGE metrics (Table \ref{tab:main_results}). This suggests that while MMedPO optimizes for general medical language style through clinical relevance weighting, it may inadvertently sacrifice fine-grained factual precision. In contrast, CaMedPO explicitly constructs preference pairs based on lesion-masked counterfactuals. This forces the model to distinguish between visual evidence and background priors, ensuring that the generated entities (captured by RadGraph) are grounded in actual image content rather than spurious correlations.
Analysis of Complex Relations. The advantage of CaMedPO is consistent in the stricter Complete-F1 metric (21.42 vs. 15.68 for MMedPO$^{\mathrm{s}}$), which requires correct identification of both entities and their dependency relations. This confirms that our causal framework not only detects abnormalities but also accurately describes their attributes and locations (e.g., distinguishing "left lung" from "right lung"), a capability that is crucial for trustworthy medical diagnosis.

Validation of Causal Mechanism. To explicitly verify that CaMedPO mitigates background shortcuts, we conducted controlled interventions at test time (Table \ref{tab:causal_validation}).
1) Robustness to Background Shifts: We randomly perturbed non-lesion regions (background swap) during inference. MMedPO suffers a significant performance drop (e.g., ROUGE-L drops from 30.13 to 25.21), indicating it relies heavily on spurious background cues. In contrast, CaMedPO maintains high performance (33.65 ROUGE-L), confirming that it has successfully learned to focus on lesion evidence rather than context.
2) Lesion-Dependency: As shown in Appendix [ref], when lesions are removed from the input, CaMedPO's performance drops more sharply than baselines, proving it is not "guessing" from the background.
3) Impact of Segmentation: We further analyzed the impact of segmentation quality (Fig. \ref{fig:seg_analysis}). While CaMedPO is robust to minor errors, better segmentation (MedSAM vs. MedKLIP) further enhances the causal contrast, validating our pipeline design.

\subsection{Ablation Study}
In Table \ref{tab:ablation}, we select SFT-enhanced MMedPO as the CaMedPO baselines to conduct ablation studies on VQA-RAD and IU-XRay datasets. Multi-rounds training, sample ratio of causal pairs, and compatibility analysis on other LVLMs are in Appendix \ref{supp:cases}.

\noindent\textbf{Loss Function Variants.}
We evaluate several loss function variants to understand their contribution. Removing the standard DPO term leads to a clear performance drop (for example, VQA-RAD open-ended recall decreases from 42.00\% to 29.50\%), indicating that learning from factual preference pairs remains essential. Applying causal weights to the original DPO loss, where the details is in Appendix \ref{supp:cases} improves over MMedPO but remains weaker than the full model, as reflected by the IU-XRay ROUGE-L score of 28.40\% compared 
with 34.13\%. Removing the counterfactual clinical query provides only marginal improvements, thus it is not adopted in our causal preference optimization.

\section{Qualitative Analysis}
To qualitatively assess the clinical reliability of CaMedPO, we examine representative examples comparing its outputs with those produced by LLaVA-Med and MMedPO. More cases are in Appendix \ref{supp:cases}. As illustrated in Fig. \ref{fig:case}, LLaVA-Med produces an erroneous description, incorrectly suggesting bilateral pleural effusions in the report generation task. MMedPO improves factual accuracy but still omits clinically relevant details and exhibits partially incomplete reasoning. In contrast, CaMedPO provides a more comprehensive and clinically aligned report. It correctly identifies normal cardiac size, mediastinal contours, and clear lung fields, fully matching the ground-truth interpretation and achieving the highest ROUGE score.
